Tierra Dorada

Environmental communication developed with young leaders in mining communities.

ClientUSAID Colombia
RoleDesigner and field communicator
Contribution Communications training and support in creating artistic products.

Introduction

Communication tools for community leaders

Tierra Dorada was an initiative led by Chemonics, Arteaga Latam, and CORCRESER to reduce the environmental impact of informal mining in the Serranía de San Lucas, Bajo Cauca in Antioquia, and the San Juan subregion of Chocó. I joined when the visual identity was already well developed and became part of the communications team. My work focused on designing and structuring workshops that gave local leaders conceptual and creative tools to communicate the environmental challenges in their municipalities.

Context

Different territories, different ways of communicating

The intervention took place in remote rural areas marked by informal mining and complex security dynamics. We worked directly with young leaders, artists, musicians and local cultural collectives. Because of the strong cultural diversity between communities, the workshops couldn’t follow a rigid format and had to adapt to the languages, rhythms and forms of expression of each municipality.

Research

Immersion in the territory and direct listening with young leaders

The research involved fieldwork with art collectives and young leaders in the municipalities where we worked. Through interviews, observation, and active listening, we learned about their everyday forms of expression and the communication channels they used in their communities before defining the approach for each workshop.

Decisions

Sharing tools instead of imposing finished materials

The central decision wasn’t a visual one; it was about defining the designer’s role in the territory. Rather than producing finished pieces from a desk, I helped train young leaders and gave them conceptual and creative tools to communicate what they were learning about the environment. I acted as a bridge between the consortium’s guidelines and local realities, facilitating the process rather than acting as the sole author, and prioritizing community ownership over strict control of the visual materials.

Result

Environmental campaigns created with local young people

The workshops led to the creation of murals, songs, poems, and audiovisual pieces about protecting water, soil, and air in mining areas. The young leaders took an active role, not just as participants but as spokespeople and content creators in their municipalities. The approach gave communities a way to develop environmental messages through their own cultural expressions.

Reflections

Designing in response to the realities of each territory

What I value most about my time at Arteaga Latam is having worked directly with communities. For me, design becomes meaningful when it goes beyond the screen and supports organizations working for social and environmental change. Working with these young people challenged me. Hearing their stories of leadership in contexts where taking on that role can cost you your life taught me about determination. Despite the difficult conditions around them, they brought joy, inspiration, and a strong commitment to their messages. I came away understanding that my work doesn’t belong only in digital spaces; it can also support the voices of people working to transform their communities.
